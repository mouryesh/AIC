# Results — Round 2 predictive/robustness upgrade

> **Every number on this page is a simulated prototype result on synthetic data.** Generated by `python -m rippletwin.evaluation.report` from tables written by `python -m rippletwin.evaluation.run_round2`, so it cannot drift from the evidence. See [docs/RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) for the original flagship localisation/detection tables this document does not repeat, and [docs/ROUND2_SCORECARD.md](ROUND2_SCORECARD.md) for the requirement-by-requirement map.

## 1. Early bottleneck prediction — lead time

- Faulted episodes evaluated: **16** (9 missed, 56% miss rate)
- **Station-confirmed** lead time — first *correctly-localised* elevated state (n=7): mean **-56.4 min**, median **-67.6 min**, min **-144.5 min**
- **Raw risk-elevation** lead time — first elevated state at *any* station, not required to name the right one (n=10): mean **-35.7 min**, median **14.9 min**
- False-alarm rate (any elevated state, any episode): **27.3%** (on clean episodes only: **100.0%**)

**Read the gap between those two numbers as the finding, not as noise.** The raw risk-elevation *median* is positive (a rising trend is often sensed with real lead time), but its *mean* is pulled negative by a handful of episodes where nothing rises until very late, and the *station-confirmed* number is worse than either — the confirmation requirement itself costs real time it cannot always make back before the constraint binds. Individual episodes with positive station-confirmed lead time do exist (see `early_warning_raw.csv`); the aggregate is honestly weak on this general corpus. The corpus mixes the dedicated long-ramp scenario (`S6_EARLY_WARNING`, designed to leave room for early warning) with a general random-episode corpus, most of which ramp in over 15-60 minutes — too fast for the persistence/localisation-confirmation requirement to clear before the fault already binds. `tests/test_early_warning.py` pins the positive case directly on `S6_EARLY_WARNING`; this table is the honest, unfiltered average across a harder, more general mix. Lead time is measured against `evaluate_metrics.true_bottleneck_onset` — a tighter, model-internal reference than the plant-board-visibility proxy the flagship lead-time metric uses (which the original build found too under-powered to headline; see RESULTS.md §5). Misses are reported, not excluded.

## 2. Predictive defect risk

- Episodes: **14** (14 contained at least one real defect)
- Model: precision **0.005**, recall **0.014**, F1 **0.008**, Brier **0.0161**
- Naive historical-rate baseline: precision **0.006**, recall **0.046**
- Stations with zero predictive coverage (MANUAL tier, no process telemetry at all): **10**

**Stated plainly: precision is weak at any operating point that also catches a useful fraction of real defects.** Defects are rare — on the order of 1e-3 per vehicle-visit at nominal — so class imbalance is severe. This is reported as measured, not tuned to look better; see `docs/LIMITATIONS.md` item 12.
